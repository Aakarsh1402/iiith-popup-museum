# Interview: G. Muralidhar (Librarian, since 2005)

> Source file: `raw/interviews_corpus/muralidhar librarian interview.docx` (converted to text for reading; the original is the authority).

The IIITH library was established alongside the institute itself in room
Nilgiri 119 (now Saranga Hall). G. Muralidhar, the current Librarian,
has been serving since 2005, by which time the library had already
relocated to the Vindhya A6 block (the shift occurred in 2001).
Initially, only the ground floor was utilized for the library, while the
first floor served as the UG1 workspace. Over the years, as the user
base grew, the book collection expanded as well; in 2016, the upper
floor was incorporated into the main library, and in 2020, the remainder
of the first floor was converted into a reading room. The library has
been a pioneer in integrating technology from its inception, initially
using LibSys 4 and subsequently transitioning over time to the latest
LibSys 10.

The library has consistently used student ID cards alongside book cards
as its issuing mechanism, never issuing dedicated library cards. The
RFID tagging of books was an initiative launched by the library in 2018,
with student assistance enlisted to expedite the tagging process. For
digital learning, six desktop computers were initially installed on the
ground floor, which later expanded to 11 systems as the library extended
its footprint to the upper floor. Previously, Ping magazine was archived
in the library as the institute's official publication; in the digital
age, it has transitioned entirely to an online blog. Book exhibitions
organized by the library were held annually from 2004 to 2011, shifted
to a biennial schedule from 2012 to 2016, and have been organized every
semester since 2016. These exhibitions have helped the library
consistently improve the quality of its acquisitions.

Book digitization began well before the COVID-19 pandemic. During that
period, publishers granted complimentary access under longstanding
agreements with the institute, enabling these digital collections to be
made available free of charge over the campus VPN. The library began in
its earliest days with an initial collection of roughly 400 books,
expanded to 5,000 volumes during its operation in Nilgiri, and by 2026
holds nearly 50,000 volumes. Furthermore, upon the completion of the New
Academic Block (NAB), the library is slated to relocate there. Recent
initiatives undertaken by the library include the addition of a
dedicated meeting room and individual reading cubicles on the upper
floor. Throughout its history, institute faculty have taken an active
interest in the library's operations, beginning with the late Prof. R.
Govindarajulu and continuing to Prof. Aniket Alam from the Human
Sciences Research Center, who has headed the library committee for over
six years.
