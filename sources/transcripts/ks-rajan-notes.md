## 1. The campus and Gachibowli area around 2006

- When he joined in 2006 the IT corridor was at the **"end of phase one"**. It was no longer the very beginning.
- **Cyber Towers** (HITEC City) opened in **1998**, with only about **6 companies** at first.
- Around **2002**, companies began moving toward the Gachibowli side.
- A **2007–08 study for the state government**, which he advised on, counted **close to 1,000 companies**. That jump from 6 to about 1,000 in 10 years was the main finding.
- **Before 2010 the area around campus was almost empty:**
  - Not much beyond IIIT and **CMC** [unclear: "Triple I.T.C.M.C."], plus a few houses (possibly the Indira Nagar area).
  - **Reliance Fresh**, and a **Spencer's** opposite it (Spencer's has since closed), appeared around **2007–08**. Reliance Digital came much later.
  - Other activity in the area only slowly began around **2010**.
- **Roads:**
  - In the **1990s** Gachibowli could only be reached from **Mehdipatnam** by **one district road**, which was also the only way to the University of Hyderabad (said as "University of Andhra Pradesh").
  - Past **Tolichowki**, there was almost nothing until IIIT, just an odd building or two.
  - After 2002, the biggest change was that the road had become **four-lane**.
- The team said their **Google Maps timelines** show the same pattern.

## 2. Basic utilities: water, power, roads

- They came from the **standard city authorities**.
- **Water:** the city water board, **HMWSSB** (said as "hydropower" [unclear]), is the main supplier.
- **Power:** an **electricity substation sits on IIIT's own land**. The land is leased to the power utility and is the institute's main power source.
  - Background: the land post-ISB and around the Microsoft area was mostly **large rocky plots** when the area was set aside for IT.
- **Roads** were handled the standard way.

## 3. Old maps, satellite images, campus layouts

- He **may have digitised campus maps** from before 2010. Students he asked digitised the campus, and the files are with one of his older students or archives. **Follow up with him.**
- **Satellite imagery is limited for that period:**
  - Google Maps has about 2005, then jumps to about 2010.
  - There was no public high-resolution data then. Only private companies sold it.
  - Other datasets, including the institute's, are **10–30 m resolution**, too coarse to see buildings.

## 4. How office systems worked before digital tools

- It was **all paper files**. He showed the team the government-style cloth/card file folders.
- About **8–9 years ago (around 2017)** work started moving one process at a time into **IMS** (the institute management system).
- Most paperwork now goes through IMS, but **some records are still kept as hard copies** because official and audit rules require it.

## 5. Government rules vs. the need for speed: do they clash?

- **His view: they don't really clash.** Rules exist to make operations smoother.
- National laws and regulator guidelines (**UGC, AICTE**) apply as they are. The institute either adopts them or adds sub-clauses to make them clearer.
- **Example, admissions:** AICTE approval has to be in place before admissions. IIIT **plans and prepares in parallel** rather than waiting, so approvals arrive before the exam or the actual activity starts.
- **The only real friction** comes when a rule changes partway through, which is rare.
- Rule changes are always **prospective, never retroactive**. Example: if the curriculum changes, students already partway through are not made to redo courses.

## 6. Do old documents still exist?

- **Yes.** Important documents are **under lock and key**, and many are now **also digital**.
- He separated three kinds of documents:
  1. **Founding or primary documents**, which are the ones that matter historically: the **Memorandum of Association** setting up the institute and the **land/campus ownership** papers.
  2. **Grants**, which run for fixed periods and expire. Old ones aren't useful.
  3. **Later utility permissions** (for example, extra power capacity as buildings grow). These are routine. He compared them to the first electricity connection number on a house: a primary record, but not meaningful history.
- **Committee minutes** are kept in the **offices that own them**, not with him:
  - Academic, thesis, PG and curriculum matters → **Academic Office**
  - Institute-level decisions → **Director's Office**
  - Research → **R&D Office**
- **Public archives online:** **curriculum documents** from **before 2008**, **2008**, and **2018** (the current one) are online. They are good for a "then vs. now" comparison of curriculum structure.
- **Physical exhibit item:** the team asked for a **blank government-style file folder** to display (most students had never seen one). He said he'd **ask someone in the office**.

## 7. If the exhibit could show one thing about IIIT's history

He gave two options, both symbolic rather than about infrastructure:

**a) The original building, where the first classes were held**
- Almost everything in the early years started in one building: first classes, all offices, and the first academic office.
- Until about **2007** all faculty sat there. From **2007 to 2010**, labs were built and moved lab by lab into a newer building.
- For the first batches, that original building is the one they recognise.
- **[unclear]** Whisper garbled the building names ("In Chile… naked", "moving to India"). The second building is probably **Vindhya**. **Check which building held the first classes.**

**b) The dual degree programme (research focus)**
- It set IIIT apart: the **first dual degree** let **4-year B.Tech students convert to the 5-year** research dual degree.
- It was planned around **2000–2001**, before he joined.
- Earlier, a **large number** of students converted. Today it's **single digits**.
- He called it a key milestone in IIIT's turn toward research. **PJ** (P. J. Narayanan) would be the best person to ask, and the team already has an interview scheduled with him.

He would **not lead with infrastructure**, because buildings keep changing as the institute grows.

## 8. A rule or culture from the early days that has lasted

- **Consultative decision-making.** Decisions aren't made by one or two people. Opinions are gathered widely first.
- Early on the institute was small enough that everyone took part directly. Now it works through **representatives, committees, and chairs**.
- Scale has changed how it works, but the consultative style has stayed.
