# Interview: Mr. Gopalakrishnan (Gopi), guest house caretaker

> Source file: `raw/interviews_corpus/gopalakrishnan(gopi) interview.docx` (converted to text for reading; the original is the authority).

Full transcript:

The journey of IIIT Hyderabad began when N. Chandrababu Naidu allotted
100 acres of land for the campus. In those early days, the entire
institute was housed in just two buildings: Nilgiri and Vindhya.

In 1998, Gachibowli was a village and forest-like area with no
significant traffic, where bullock carts used to wander in the path near
the campus. The first batch had an intake of 50 students, but 1 student
did not join, leaving 49.

The Chairman was Prof. Raj Reddy, and the Director was Prof. Narendra
Ahuja.

In the second year, the intake was 100 students, of whom 99 actually
joined. To cater to them, the construction of the Old Boys Hostel (OBH)
began during the second year.

Its first floor served as a mess facility, and during the college's
third year, another mess was set up on the ground floor. The third year
also marked the construction of the New Boys Hostel (NBH) and the NBH
Kadamba mess. These messes were run on a contract basis. Before their
construction was completed, the guest house served as the campus mess.

Later, Director Narendra Ahuja relocated back to the USA, and Prof.
Rajeev Sangal from the University of Hyderabad (UoH) took over as the
new Director.

In those early years, wild animals such as bears, pigs, and monkeys used
to stray in from the nearby forest-like surroundings. To keep them out,
the campus administration decided to build a boundary wall. However,
local villagers broke down sections of the wall to let their domestic
cattle, such as cows, enter the campus to graze. Police intervention was
ultimately required to resolve the issue; the animals were rounded up
and relocated, and the villagers were sternly warned.

During the institute's initial years, the 1st Afro-Asian Games were
hosted at the nearby Gachibowli GMC Balayogi Stadium, and the official
press meets for the event were conducted inside the IIITH guest house.
Later, owing to the expansion needs of the Balayogi Stadium, 30 acres of
the institute's land had to be surrendered to them.

Regarding the original buildings: Vindhya (which had previously served
as the district collectorate) and Nilgiri (which had housed the district
court) were the structures handed over by Chandrababu Naidu within the
100-acre parcel to launch the college.

Companies such as Motorola, IBM, Oracle, and Satyam Computers set up
corporate development centres right on campus. Later, as these companies
built or acquired their own premises in Hyderabad, they gradually
vacated the campus. Until their departure, however, members of their
managerial and technical leadership actively participated in designing
the academic curriculum and courses. Industry professionals like Prof.
Govindarajulu from IBM chose to remain on campus, transitioning into
full-time faculty roles. A similar path was followed by professionals
from Oracle.

Prof. Raj Reddy was eventually succeeded by Prof. Ashok Jhunjhunwala
from IIT Madras; distinguished professors and directors from
institutions like IISc and IIT Madras also regularly assisted in shaping
the curriculum. Satyam Computers maintained an active presence on campus
until around the time the corporate scam surfaced.

As for the story of the campus telephone booth: a retired Commodore
serving as an administrative official on campus had initially asked Mr.
Gopi to manage the guest house mess. Feeling that running the mess was
not a one-man job, Mr. Gopi opted instead to run a public telephone
booth inside the campus. However, the government telecom officials
responsible for setting up the connection kept delaying the work.
Eventually, an official demanded a bribe of ₹2,000 from Mr. Gopi to
expedite the setup. Coming from a simple rural background, he paid the
amount, yet the booth remained uninstalled. When the Commodore learned
of this, his military discipline took over; infuriated, he lodged a
direct complaint with senior government authorities, and the phone booth
was commissioned that very day. Once operational, the booth catered to
around 100 people daily, witnessing long queues particularly between
8:00 PM and midnight when calling rates were significantly cheaper.

Later, around 2002, with the rise of mobile phones, the telephone booth
was eventually dismantled. Mr. Gopi transitioned into the role of
caretaker at the guest house, where he has continued to serve ever
since.
